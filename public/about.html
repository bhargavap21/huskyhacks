Second Look

Concept for HuskyHack 2026 · synthetic data Back to the demo

What Second Look is

Protection members turn on inside Sound’s own app, with Sound employees able to step in before money moves. A concept for HuskyHack 2026, “Stop the Scam Before the Money Moves”. Everything here is a simulation on the kit’s synthetic data. The member app is a concept mock, not Sound software.

Member story

Taken from the kit’s synthetic events. The outcome is a hypothesis for the pilot to measure, not a result.

Linda (M1001) is told her grandson is in jail and that she must keep it secret. In the kit, she wires $8,500 in a branch: it was held at 14:21, an employee reached her at 15:42, she confirmed (her right), and it was released at 17:04. Recovery was unsuccessful. Our hypothesis: putting the evidence on the hold and starting a guided conversation at the moment of initiation could change what a member decides. The pilot is how we would measure that.

Trigger

Thresholds are proposals. Rules use only fields in the events file, and only events at or before the payment. The Member Key is never read by the app.

  1. R1. New recipient, a wire, no wire in 365+ days and 3x the usual amount puts the payment on hold. Linda: 2,411 days, 4.77x.
  2. R2. A password reset or contact change, a new recipient and a payment within 7 days means verify first, with confirmation sent to the pre-change contact details. The pattern alone does not prove a takeover.
  3. R3. Three or more growing P2P payments to one new recipient, then a first-ever wire or external transfer, bring a banner in context and human outreach.
  4. R4. Large branch cash soon after a limits question starts a private branch conversation. It uses the kit’s employee-recorded call topic, which the data dictionary marks “partly” observable.

Intervention and the role of people

Within Sound’s capability assumptions only. For a member’s own payment, Sound delays and advises, and the member decides.

  • Hold a wire, ACH or external transfer for up to 4 hours.
  • Intervene before submit on P2P, because it can’t be recalled.
  • Start a branch conversation for cash.
  • Stop a payment only after a verified account takeover.
  • Employees see a queue sorted by hold time left, the field evidence, a conversation guide, and audited actions.
  • At 4 hours a held payment sends only if the member reconfirmed; otherwise it returns unsent with notice. That disposition is a proposal to validate with Sound.

False positives, override and appeal

Two legitimate members from the kit, and how they get through quickly.

  • M1057’s legitimate $51,586.52 closing wire trips R1 exactly like Linda’s. The answer is a fast path: the member confirms (step-up code to on-file details) or an employee calls and releases, in minutes rather than the 3-4 hours in the kit.
  • M1037’s $1,188.37 P2P to a grandchild never gets held. One banner, one tap.
  • Dismissals are recorded as member feedback, never as silent training.

What it misses

On the kit data, 7 scam journeys pass every rule. We show one of them rather than hide it.

M1102 wired $5,000 for a “federal agent” after asking about limits. The wire wasn’t unusual enough for R1, and R4 covers cash only. Only the standard banner showed.

Data used

Only fields Sound already has about the payment and the account.

  • Used: payment amount, rail, channel, timing, new-recipient indicators, days since the last same-rail payment, amount vs baseline, recent profile change and password reset, and trusted-contact status.
  • Not used: device intelligence, biometrics, voice, carrier or consortium data, or members’ texts, calls, email or browsing.

Alerts without fatigue

Quiet by default. Interruptions only for money about to move.

  • A banner appears in place, inside the payment flow.
  • Budgets, cooldowns and quiet hours apply to member interruptions, never to the hold clock or the employee queue.

90-day pilot

Assumptions, not data. No fraud rates come from the synthetic data, which has 41% victims by design.

  1. Offer opt-in to a defined segment. Never restrict by age alone.
  2. Train branch and contact-center staff on the guide. The fraud team owns the queue.
  3. Set baselines at launch: opt-in rate, holds per 1,000 payments, hold-to-release time, staff minutes per hold.
  4. Measure harm prevented, scam completion, how soon recovery starts, warning completion, satisfaction, false-positive rate, time to release for legitimate members (target: minutes), time to intervention, employee effort, and after-hours holds waiting.

Privacy, accessibility, responsible design

No “safe” or “scam” verdicts and no accuracy claims.

  • Opt-in, revocable in one tap, no dark patterns.
  • Trusted contacts are voluntary, revocable and member-chosen. They are informed only, never given access or control.
  • The copy allows for the possibility that the exploiter is a relative or caregiver.
  • Plain language, large type, 44px targets, AA contrast, screen-reader labels.

Limits

Concept demo on synthetic data.

  • Rules are unvalidated proposals.
  • The chat is not a validated coaching tool.
  • Holds and stops affect only the simulation.
  • An optional, member-consented email companion is out of scope for this prototype.
